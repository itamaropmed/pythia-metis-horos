# Out-of-distribution MAE floor audit (2026-09-13)

## Frozen design and estimand

The ten new generators are in `horos/benchmark/ood_mae_designs.py`:
beta-Gaussian covariates, separated covariate support with Laplace noise,
oscillatory center, V-shaped center, radial two-dimensional center,
three-dimensional max center, sparse 20D, heteroskedastic Gaussian,
heteroskedastic Laplace, and shifted exponential skew. Their conditional
medians, MAE floors, and median-Lipschitz caps are analytic and are evaluated
at the *actual sampled covariates*, not at an unrelated population average.
An additional generator test checks empirical MAE against the analytic floor
on 20,000 fresh cases per family and verifies the declared Lipschitz
inequality on sampled feature pairs. This catches a false known-noise target
before it enters a method comparison.

The loss is MAE. The estimand for bounds is finite-design average conditional
Bayes MAE. Sampling entities are iid by construction and pair/group geometry
uses covariates only. Gaussian, Laplace, and exponential outcomes have no
finite raw outcome cap, so both methods clip a nonnegative lower witness on
the frozen grid `(0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6)` and Bonferroni-adjust across
the grid and Hoeffding/Bernstein methods. Grouped also adjusts across group
sizes. Every bound has nominal one-sided 95% confidence under the exact DGP
assumptions; no predictor is fitted inside Pythia.

## Bound comparison

Run `run_ood_mae_comparison(seed=20260928, repetitions=20,
n_entities=1600)` from `horos/benchmark/ood_mae_comparison.py`.

| Method | Below known risk | Median usable tightness | Paired wins |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Clipped paired 95% | 200/200 | 0.5634 | 0 |
| Clipped grouped 95% | 200/200 | **0.6405** | **180** |

Twenty draws tied at zero on sparse 20D. The 200/200 coverage count has a
95% Wilson lower bound of 0.9812. The grouped method won all 20 draws in
each of the other nine families. This is a strong within-benchmark result
against the implemented paired Hoeffding/Bernstein baseline, **not** a claim
that it beats all published bounds or that the smoothness cap can be known on
real clinical data. The earlier independent seed (`20260925`, 10 draws per
family) showed the same pattern: 90 grouped wins, zero paired wins, ten ties.

## Point-estimator comparison

The 1,600-entity grouped witness center is not a competitive estimator.
The local empirical-loss jackknife had median absolute error 0.0079 against
known risk; external leave-one-out kNN had 0.0050 and won 142/200 draws.
At `n=192`, a global constrained-omniscient LP and its outcome-blind
half-sample jackknife are computationally feasible. The latter optimizes
case-level actions but fits no deployable predictive model. It is inspired
by [Anderson–Bjarnadottir (2024)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0296904),
using all pair constraints and oracle-declared `L`, **not** their exact
threshold/link protocol. It is a point-estimation heuristic, not a valid
95% lower bound. The external kNN comparator *does* fit a predictive rule.

On a fresh seed (`20260929`, 20 draws per family, `n=192`):

| Point estimator | Median absolute error to known floor |
|---|---:|
| LP half-sample jackknife | **0.0161** |
| Raw omniscient LP | 0.0219 |
| External OOF kNN | 0.0232 |
| Group local-loss jackknife | 0.0412 |

The LP jackknife was closer than the raw LP in 136/200 draws, an exploratory
one-sided sign-test `p=1.95e-7` for this fixed benchmark. Against kNN it won
only 109/200 (`p=0.115`), so **estimator superiority is not established**.
It failed badly on max-feature and sparse-20D designs; the aggregate median
alone must not hide those failures. The earlier independent seed (`20260927`)
gave LP jackknife 0.0167 versus kNN 0.0265, with a similarly modest 111–89
paired win count. No retrospective seed search is being promoted to a
confirmatory claim.

## Mayo preservation and release status

`tests/test_guarded.py` still reproduces Arm B guarded MAE bound
28.8495996043 minutes, calibrated floor estimator 31.1186421201 minutes,
best frozen OOF MAE 32.8700117033 minutes, and the conditional Qcap rung
24.1867259409 minutes. The new code does not alter the guard or patient data.
`tests/test_mayo_decision_losses.py` pins aggregate grouped W30/pinball
retrospective outputs. None of the synthetic success upgrades the Mayo
calibrated result to `proved` or establishes real-world smoothness.

Next gates: compare against the exact Anderson–Bjarnadottir protocol and
other published floor estimators on matched data; audit the group action
smoothness and provenance on Mayo; validate point-estimator bias across new
families and larger sample sizes; and reject or downgrade sparse designs
where a bound remains zero. No universal-best claim is warranted.
