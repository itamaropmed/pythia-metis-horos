\section{The exact scope of the certificate}
Fix covariates $z_{1:n}$, disjoint outcome-blind groups, conditionally independent entity outcomes, a metric that extends to any virtual group centre, and a selected loss-specific Bayes action $a^*(z)$ satisfying $|a^*(z)-a^*(z')|\le Ld(z,z')$ on the audited design. The pair and group statistics are pointwise no larger than the loss of these Bayes actions. Their conditional expected scores therefore lie below $n^{-1}\sum_i r_\ell(z_i)$. One-sided concentration plus a union bound over the predeclared candidate registry yields a conditional finite-design lower confidence bound. It is not automatically a future-population or clinical guarantee.

\paragraph{What an external $L$ audit would require.} For median and pinball, suppose conditional cdfs obey $\sup_t|F_z(t)-F_{z'}(t)|\le Kd(z,z')$ and densities are at least $m>0$ between their unique $\tau$-quantiles. Then $|q_\tau(z)-q_\tau(z')|\le (K/m)d(z,z')$ by cdf inversion. An independent scientific or held-out-reference audit of $K$ and $m$ could justify $L=K/m$; choosing $L$ after reading the evaluation outcomes cannot. No such bounds exist yet for Mayo. The max-mass centre for a window loss may jump discontinuously at a mode switch, so the quantile argument does \emph{not} transfer to W30. Until an external window-action cap is available, its Mayo bound is only a numerical sensitivity diagnostic.

\paragraph{Implemented pinball score.} The implemented radial group relaxation is asymmetric:
\[
\tilde\ell_\tau(y,a;\rho)=\tau[y-a-\rho]_+ +(1-\tau)[a-y-\rho]_+.
\]
If $|a^\dagger-a^*(z_i)|\le\rho_i$, each of the two positive parts is bounded by the corresponding positive part of the true residual. Thus the relaxed pinball score is at most true pinball loss pointwise. The previous main-text formula $\min(\tau,1-\tau)[|y-a|-\rho]_+$ described a valid but weaker statistic and did not match code; the revised manuscript corrects it.

\paragraph{Fixed-group range.} For outcomes in a population-valid support interval of span $R$ and group size $k$, the exact worst-case \emph{unrelaxed} empirical pinball optimum is
\[
C_{k,\tau}=R\max_{m=0,\ldots,k}\min\left\{\frac{\tau(k-m)}{k},\frac{(1-\tau)m}{k}\right\}.
\]
The relaxed score is no larger. The empirical optimum is a convex function of the outcome vector, so its maximum over the support cube occurs at an endpoint vector; $m$ counts lower endpoints. Coincident group features and $L=0$ attain the constant. For $\tau=0.1$, $C_{2,\tau}/R=0.05$, $C_{4,\tau}/R=0.075$, and $C_{10,\tau}/R=0.09$. The simpler $\tau(1-\tau)R$ is a valid universal cap and sharp as group size varies, but it is not necessarily attained by a \emph{fixed} finite group. The earlier judge's sharpness approval applied to the two-atom population law, not to every empirical group size.

\paragraph{Shared-error correction.} Subadditivity of an error dispersion such as MAD does not, by itself, imply a lower bound on Bayes risk. Identical zero predictors have the same zero disagreement when $Y=0$ almost surely (Bayes MAE $0$) and when $Y$ is equally likely to be $-M$ or $M$ (Bayes MAE $M$). The revised paper replaces the unsupported shared-bias-to-Bayes-risk proposition with this non-identifiability counterexample.

\section{Known-risk MAE benchmark: every design, not just the headline}
The frozen seed is 20260928; ten analytic-noise designs, 20 independent draws each, 1,600 entities per draw. Each entry in Table~S1 is a median over that design's 20 draws. The gold column is the median \emph{analytic conditional Bayes MAE at the sampled covariates}; the method columns are median one-sided nominal-95\% bounds. Group and pair use their own Bonferroni-controlled clip/method registries. The final column counts strict group wins out of 20. Bold identifies the larger bound within each row; the gold is a target, not a competitor. All 200 grouped and paired bounds were below analytic truth in their draws; the draw-level 95\% Wilson lower limit for 200/200 is 0.9812. This empirical count does not prove uniform coverage.

Pythia wins this \emph{bound} comparison, not the point-estimator comparison.

\section{Multi-loss results and the changed route}
The disjoint five-loss registry comprises q10/q90 pinball and W15/W30/W60 miss risk on ten duration-like designs (five numeric, five deterministic pre-outcome note templates). For the earlier frozen loss-only route, seed 20261006 supplied 20 draws/design at 512 entities/draw. The route used paired Bentkus for quantiles and grouped Bentkus for windows, allocating $\alpha=0.01$ to each of the five losses per draw. All five losses were below truth jointly in 200/200 draws. On quantiles the route \emph{ties} paired Bentkus by construction, whereas it improves W15 and W30 in 177/200 and 127/200 draws respectively. W60 has zero Bayes miss floor throughout this registry and contributes uninformative ties.

\section{Retrospective Mayo is an application, not the win claim}
All entries use the same 10,528 earliest-per-patient cases in four chronological panels, with illustrative $L=300$ and a claimed population support span of 900 minutes that has not been externally established. The main manuscript's W30 table is the \emph{legacy radial/adaptive} calculation, not the later geometry route. The frozen OOF comparator is MAE-selected retrospectively and is not established as a deployed scheduling model.

A separate actual-duration injection produced zero oracle error and a public certification refusal. No patient text or row-level outcome is reproduced here.
