# PYTHIA-G3: three-gap certificates and per-sample risk labels (development, 2026-10-17)

- Code: `pythia/core/global_bound.py`, plus `anchored_information.py` and `multiscale_pair_extrapolation.py`.
- Literature: `/home/claude/reports/Certified risk bounds tabular text.md` (2022–2026 survey).

## The task

**Input.** n records (table row X, note T, outcome Y), a loss ℓ, and optionally a prediction model f.

**Output.** Simultaneous certificates (α = 0.05), with no model needed for the floor sides:

| Quantity | Meaning | Side | Assumption |
|---|---|---|---|
| R\*_ℓ(X) | best risk of any rule using the table | two-sided | none: cells are exact replicates |
| R\*_ℓ(X,T) | best risk of any rule using table + text: *what all the models in the world can do* | **upper** | none: neighbour-label leave-one-out lemma, any loss |
| | | lower | A(½), multiscale extrapolation. Theorem 4 says no assumption-free version exists |
| Data / information gap R\*(X) − R\*(X,T) | value of the text beyond the table | lower | none (AFI contrast) |
| Estimation gap R(f) − R\*(X,T) | headroom any model could still gain over f | lower: none; upper: A(½) | |
| Distribution gap | finite n, unknown P | the interval widths | |
| Per-sample labels | irreducibility score (model-free), model uncertainty, categories, certified "easy" group | group-level guarantee via Learn-then-Test | exchangeability |

**Loss-agnostic machinery**
- **Any loss, upper bound.** The neighbour-label lemma: a predictor built from *other* rows' labels is independent of Y_i, so its risk is at least R\*.
- **Any loss with an exact in-cell minimiser, table lower bound.** Fixed-action domination.
- **Transfers.**
  - Log-loss floor ≥ 2 × the Brier/2 floor.
  - Conditional mutual information ≥ ½ the Brier information (Pinsker, plus ℓ₂ ≤ ℓ₁).
  - f_K(Ḡ) ≤ R₀₁ ≤ Ḡ.

**Validity.** 0 violations in 105 certificate bundles: 90 classification (6 datasets × 3 losses × 5 runs) and 15 regression (3 losses × 5 runs). That covers every bound, gap and loss.

**Tightness**
- **Brier joint ceiling:** 1.09–1.15 × the true best-any-model risk, and 1.46 × under outcome leak.
- **0-1 and log ceilings:** looser, at 1.4–1.5 ×.
- **Regression ceilings:** loose, at 1.7–3.9 ×. The declared outcome range enters the concentration term. This is the main thing to improve.

**Certified headroom.** In the synergy dataset, the certificate proves that *some* model beats logistic regression by at least 0.023 Brier. The truth is 0.059. The model cannot represent the interactions, and the bound detects that without fitting a better model.

**Weak sides**
- The γ lower floor on the joint is near 0, as Theorem 4 predicts for text.
- The certified data gap is small (≈ 0.005 against a truth of 0.04) at n = 4800 certified rows.

## Per-sample risk labels (generated data, true per-sample laws known)

| Dataset | Spearman of model-free irreducibility ĝ with true g | Spearman of model uncertainty u with true g | AUROC for the top-25% irreducible, ĝ / u |
|---|---|---|---|
| D02 | 0.43 | 0.62 | 0.67 / 0.77 |
| D04 synergy (model misspecified) | **0.46** | 0.16 | **0.67 / 0.54** |
| D09 | 0.75 | 0.88 | 0.90 / 0.95 |

**Reading**
- A well-specified model's uncertainty tracks irreducible risk better.
- The model-free score wins when the model is misspecified, which is exactly when the model's own uncertainty cannot be trusted.
- Neither score identifies *per-sample* reducible error (Spearman ≤ 0.25). This matches the literature: per-instance aleatoric/epistemic splits are not identifiable from single labels.
- Certified "easy" groups (target error ≤ half the overall error, δ = 0.05) were small on the generated data (0–43% of patients). Their **true** error stayed at or below ε.

**Per-sample labels on the held-out half (specialty).** Overall error is 33%.
- **Certified-easy group from the model-free irreducibility score:** 40–52% of notes, with realized error **9–12%** against a target of 15–18%. It was found in 3 of 3 seeds. The model-uncertainty score found one in only 1 of 3, because the fixed-sequence test stopped early.
- **"Irreducibly hard" group** (top 25% of the irreducibility score): error 53–62%.
- **"Reducible" group** (low irreducibility, high model uncertainty, 4–6% of notes): error 59–83%. These are the cases a better model could fix.

## Reading

1. **The task is sound and new.** The survey found no finite-sample, loss-agnostic certificate on the best achievable risk from table + text, and no certified "text beyond table" quantity. The best published Bayes-error bounds need soft or repeated labels and cover only 0-1-type losses.
2. **The best-any-model ceiling is tight for Brier,** and valid for every loss tested.
3. **The floor from text is the hard side,** as the no-free-lunch theorem says. Industry-grade use should report the ceiling, the table floor and the certified gains, and treat the joint floor as assumption-tagged.
4. **Per-sample labels are useful and certifiable at the group level.** The model-free irreducibility score adds the most when the model is misspecified.
5. **Next:**
   - Tighten the regression ceilings (deep-level betting bounds, better aggregators).
   - Combine ĝ and u by isotonic calibration into a single continuous risk scale.
   - Run on MIMIC with `scripts/run_vtc_mimic.py`.
