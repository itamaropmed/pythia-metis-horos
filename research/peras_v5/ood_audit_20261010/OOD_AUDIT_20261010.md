# Frozen Pythia v5.7: independent outcome-mechanism audit (2026-10-10)

## Question and decision

The Pythia backbone is **not complete as a general certified Bayes-risk lower-bound theory**. It is a promising estimator on some losses, but the current lower-bound procedure overstates the known attainable risk on several unseen mechanisms. The new OOD suite was evaluated without changing v5.7 coefficients, screening, witnesses, or bound picks after seeing outcomes.

## Protocol

- Ten prespecified new conditional-outcome mechanisms: linear Gaussian, Friedman-1 variant Gaussian, checkerboard Laplace, smooth heteroscedastic Gaussian, sparse 30-dimensional Student-t5, text interaction Laplace, Friedman-1 variant skew lognormal, text-dependent mixture, nonlinear Student-t3, and discrete-state Gamma/normal replicates.
- Three independent seeds per mechanism, 800 samples per run. The mechanism is the unit of comparison; the three seeds are summarized by their median absolute relative error.
- All methods see the same numeric columns and three cached, outcome-blind note concepts. The note vocabulary and reader are reused from the prior suite, so this is an OOD **outcome-mechanism** test, not an OOD language-understanding test. No new LLM calls were made.
- Exact conditional Bayes risks are evaluated by the project's frozen `gold.pointwise` integration for each known location-scale noise family.
- Pythia uses frozen v5.7. Rivals are the Gamma test and Rice/1NN for MSE, a fixed 20-neighbor leave-one-out estimator, a three-fold gradient-boosted plug-in, and a Cortes-style learning-curve extrapolation. These are method-family implementations, not reference-author code. The best rival is selected separately for each design using the known gold, deliberately favoring the rivals.
- Pythia performs its frozen outcome-based feature screening while rivals receive the full shared representation. This favors Pythia on sparse designs; a publication-grade point-estimator comparison must give rivals a development-selected screening option too.
- A supplemental matched `n=200` MAE comparison uses the repository's published-protocol Anderson–Bjarnadóttir LP implementation. It is a heuristic point floor, not a 95% confidence bound.
- A strict win means Pythia has lower median absolute relative error than every available rival for that mechanism. The exact one-sided sign test uses ten mechanisms, not thirty repeated draws. Five losses are reported, so individual p-values do not establish familywise significance.

## Lower-bound results

| Loss | Bound above known truth | Other failure |
|---|---:|---|
| MSE | **7/30** | 3/30 negative, with the discrete-state median bound about -22,513 against a positive risk near 464 |
| MAE | **5/30** | All three checkerboard runs fail |
| Q90 | 1/30 | Sparse high-dimensional case |
| W30 | 4/30 | Checkerboard and nonlinear heavy-tail cases |
| W60 | 0/30 | 7/30 bounds are nonpositive and therefore uninformative |

The MAE miss rate is 16.7% across these draws, and its checkerboard misses recur in every seed. The current v5.7 bound uses SIMEX extrapolation and a `z × sd × sqrt(k/n)` overlap allowance selected for past designs. Neither component gives finite-sample lower coverage under unrestricted near-replicate heterogeneity. The bound should be labelled *calibrated on the original design family* and should refuse or downgrade on this suite.

At `n=200`, Pythia is closer than the faithful Anderson–Bjarnadóttir heuristic on 9/10 design medians (one-sided sign p=0.0107). Pythia still has 3/30 MAE bound misses there and underestimates the discrete-state MAE badly (median relative error 0.555). This comparison does not validate a certificate.

## What the failures identify

1. **Local matching bias is not bounded.** Checkerboard and nonlinear surfaces create within-tuple shifts that can exceed the finite-tuple optimism; the lower witness then rises above the Bayes risk. A useful theorem needs an explicit estimable or declared modulus of conditional-law variation and a bias term in the lower bound.
2. **Outcome-driven feature screening can miss interactions.** On the 30-D design it retained `[0,1,4]` or `[0,1,4,8]` (zero-based indices including booked slot) and missed an interaction coordinate. No selection-safe proof accompanies the bound.
3. **SIMEX and curvature extrapolation are estimators, not one-sided inequalities.** Their slope and curvature estimates can move either way; they need a simultaneous uncertainty interval or must be kept out of the certified rung.
4. **The discrete-state path needs exact-replicate routing and numerical guards.** The huge negative MSE bound is a numerical failure, and the point estimate is unstable at small n. A certificate API must clip a vacuous lower endpoint to zero and refuse on failed diagnostics.
5. **The information set must be explicit.** Cached note concepts form a lossy text representation. An outcome-blind reader can miss hidden signal, so the certified estimand can only refer to the declared readable representation unless sufficiency is established.

## Reproducibility and limits

The original `pythia5.py` inside `peras_v5b.tgz` matches its `FROZEN_v57.sha256`. The audit copy changes only import paths and the witness-file path; the frozen estimator and bound equations were not edited. Source, exact row outputs, and analyzer are in this audit directory. With NumPy, SciPy, and scikit-learn installed, run `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 python3 audit_ood.py --all --reps 3 --out /private/tmp/pythia_ood_rerun.jsonl`, then `python3 analyze_ood.py --input /private/tmp/pythia_ood_rerun.jsonl`. Use a new output filename for each run. The matched LP comparison is `python3 audit_ab.py --out /private/tmp/pythia_ab_rerun.jsonl`.

This is a ten-mechanism stress test, not a representative random sample of all possible tasks. Its three seeds per mechanism give limited within-mechanism precision. The Q90 result is promising, but multiple testing, repeated use of the same note generator, unequal feature-selection options, and method implementation differences prevent a broad superiority claim. The observed bound counterexamples are sufficient to reject universal certification under the current assumptions.
